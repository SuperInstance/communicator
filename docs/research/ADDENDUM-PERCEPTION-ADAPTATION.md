# ADDENDUM - Cross-modal mathematics and perceptual adaptation (Casey, 2026-10-21)

## The same mathematics, cross-referenced
Microphone delay geometry and the ASCII room map are one subject:
the map tells the audio model where the walls are, so echoes become
PREDICTABLE - and what the map cannot predict is the event. Audio
trains faster with a mapped room because residuals carry the news.
Walk-by signatures: as Casey passes the computer mic, the loudness
ENVELOPE rises and falls - the robust signal. True Doppler pitch
shift at walking speed (~0.4 percent) is below honest threshold;
envelope is apparent and apparent is enough. Sensation turns into
perception when the modes stop reporting separately and the fused
residue is simply: he walked by, toward, away, is still.

## The adaptation law (the boots)
New boots: an inch of heel and at first EVERYTHING is effortful -
coffee lands wrong, perspective shifts, gait stiff. Over a day the
differences compress into muscle memory: this is where my normal
is now. A shirt is felt for seconds, then never again - you feel
the shoulder rub THROUGH it, fingers on shoulder, not shirt twice.
So with every mode in this system:
  attention -> calibration -> transparency.
A new sensor starts as an object of attention (raw, expensive,
cross-checked constantly). Calibration against the other modes
bakes its corrections in. It ends as a transparent instrument:
the system does not feel its microphones; it feels the person
walking past them. Per-context normals (this room, this mic, this
gait, these shoes) are the baked state - calculators for perception.

## The guardrail
Adaptation can bake in a limp. A normal adopted without verification
is the silent-audio failure wearing perceptions clothes: everything
felt fine through a dead channel. So normals are VERIFIED, on a
schedule, by the self-checks - the system touches its own shirt on
purpose, forever, to prove the shirt is not what it is feeling.

## Perception is the lens, understood (Casey, 2026-10-21)
Perception is what you notice, and it improves NOT by noticing more
things but by understanding the lens of observation - what the
instrument does to the world on its way in. The blind mans cane:
day in and day out, the tip becomes a finger - he does not think
about vibrations up the stick, he thinks "sidewalk edge". And his
hearing is cultivated, not gifted: he locates passers-by in sounds
the sighted never register, because his model of what his ears do
is exact. Engineering consequences:
- LENS FILES: every sensor gets a living model of itself - delays,
  drifts, deadbands, failure signatures - calibrated, versioned.
  The perception layer never reads a sensor raw; it reads the
  world THROUGH the lens file.
- The improvement metric is lens accuracy (residual after the lens
  is accounted), not channel count: another camera adds nothing
  until its lens is understood.
- NOTICING = prediction error through a known lens: the system
  notices what its calibrated instruments cannot explain. That is
  why the shading layer tags surprise - surprise is calibrated
  perception noticing.
- A new sensor is a stick in the hand: budget the clumsy phase;
  day-in-day-out use is the training, and there is no shortcut.
