# BRIEF - The Potato Drill (Casey via Muse, 2026-10-20)

## Source
A fine-arts-camp acting exercise Casey was taught: throw a potato to
someone; before each throw, say "I love you" to them - a DIFFERENT way
every time. Sincere. Leaving. Obligated, means nothing. Means everything.
Same three words; the message is entirely in the delivery. Caseys line:
"a person that can act it is often a person that can understand it - part
of the generative game is also part of the interpretive game."

## Why it is a corpus instrument, not an anecdote
Controlled variation: words held constant, INTENT varies, everything else
measured. The per-intent deltas in the melody (MIDI) and face channels ARE
the tone map - the thing flat text provably cannot carry (Casey, tonight,
saying "tone" five ways into the dictation: five messages, one word).

## Analysis side (yours, acceptance worktree)
In the speech-to-score report, add a drill-shaped analysis wherever a
session contains repeated same-word/same-phrase deliveries: extract each
instances note sequence (pitch contour, duration, energy) + face-segment
state, and tabulate the deltas. If no natural repetitions exist yet, say
so and spec the table the drill mode will feed.

## Page side (ZCodes queue, relayed by Muse separately)
A drill mode: one phrase on screen, a deck of intent cards (sincere /
leaving / obligated / everything / joking / quiet-furious / your deck),
each delivery auto-recorded as its own segment tagged with the intent.
